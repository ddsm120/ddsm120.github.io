Selected work / 01 / Redesign Project

A Warmer Welcome

Over two years, I redesigned the resort’s 32-page What’s On Guide and its production process, aligning it with the current brand and enabling more staff to maintain it. The redesign reduced paper use by 60% and eased the demands on shared office printers.

Project overview

Tangalooma Island Resort · Guest communications, design and production

Explore more work ↗

The challenge

The guide had evolved from a detailed A–Z reference into the resort’s What’s On Guide. Created in Microsoft Publisher and printed in black and white on coloured paper, it no longer reflected the resort’s updated brand.

Updates depended on one staff member, including corrections and last-minute tour schedule changes. Printing could take hours and cause paper jams, tying up printers used by the wider head office. The process was difficult to hand over when that staff member was away.

My role and approach

I examined production costs, staff time, paper use and the operational risk of relying on one person. I compared the existing process with a proposed redesign through a cost-benefit analysis, researching equipment options in response to department heads’ requests and assessing the viability and costs of changing production.

Before redesigning the guide, I consulted the departments that used and updated it to understand their workflows, constraints and difficulties. Stakeholder meetings, feedback and approvals shaped a brief covering both the guest-facing document and the process staff needed to maintain it. I designed around the capabilities of the staff member responsible for updates so they could continue maintaining the new design.

What I delivered

  • A redesigned guide aligned with the resort’s current brand standards.
  • A document designed for in-house printing and updates by staff without design experience.
  • An accompanying training manual explaining how to update and produce the booklet.

The outcome

Paper use fell by 60%, and printing the guide required less work. More staff could maintain the document, reducing reliance on a single person and making updates easier as tour schedules changed and events were added.

The project brought together visual design, departmental consultation and practical changes to everyday operations.
