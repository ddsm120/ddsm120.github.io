Managing school publications

As a graphic designer at PCGA, I worked on school publications throughout the year, including newsletters, prospectuses and templates. Schools were the agency’s primary clients, and yearbooks were the most intensive part of the publication calendar, with peak production running from September to December.

The scale of the work

I managed publications from initial brief to final delivery, often overseeing four yearbooks simultaneously, each ranging from 180 to 250 pages. Each school had its own branding, content requirements and production process, creating competing schedules and priorities during the end-of-year period.

Stakeholder coordination

I worked directly with school coordinators to organise content and feedback from contributors across departments. This involved:

  • Setting timelines and submission requirements.
  • Coordinating written content, images and approvals.
  • Consolidating feedback from multiple contributors.
  • Guiding each publication through revisions and sign-off.
  • Identifying delays, missing content and production risks early.

Publication management

Alongside the design work, I managed project schedules and competing priorities across the publications. I coordinated closely with printers to align production and delivery with each school’s deadlines.

The work combined publication design with sustained client communication, content coordination and print production management during a demanding seasonal workload.
